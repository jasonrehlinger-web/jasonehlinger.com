Here's the good news: getting out of debt fast isn't about earning more or finding some trick. It's about sequencing. Attack your debts in the right order, protect your momentum, and the same balances that feel permanent today start falling one by one.

## Why "Just Pay It Down" Never Works

The **external problem** is obvious: you owe money and you want it gone. But the reason it lingers is the **internal problem** underneath — the quiet dread that shows up when you open a banking app, the sense that no matter how hard you work, you're running to stay in place.

That dread is what makes people avoid the numbers entirely. And avoidance is exactly what keeps you stuck, because debt only responds to focused, consistent pressure. Spreading your extra money evenly across five balances feels responsible, but it's the slowest possible way out. You make a little progress everywhere and finish nothing, so it never *feels* like you're winning — and when it doesn't feel like winning, you quit.

The fix is to stop paying everything down at once and start knocking debts out one at a time, in a deliberate order. That's where the two proven methods come in.

<div class="pull-quote">Debt doesn't get beaten by working harder. It gets beaten by concentrating fire on one target at a time.</div>

## The Two Methods: Snowball vs. Avalanche

Every legitimate debt-payoff plan is a version of one of these. Both start the same way: you make the minimum payment on every debt, then throw every extra dollar you can find at *one* target. The only difference is which target you pick first.

### The Debt Snowball

List your debts from **smallest balance to largest**, ignoring interest rates. Attack the smallest one with everything you've got while paying minimums on the rest. When it's gone, roll that entire payment onto the next-smallest debt. Repeat.

The snowball wins on **psychology**. You get a real win fast — a whole account paid off, closed, gone — and that early victory is fuel. In the studies, people on the snowball plan are measurably more likely to actually reach zero, because momentum keeps them in the fight.

### The Debt Avalanche

List your debts from **highest interest rate to lowest**, ignoring balance size. Attack the highest-rate debt first, then roll that payment down to the next-highest rate.

The avalanche wins on **math**. It always costs you less in total interest and often gets you out a little faster on paper. On big balances — think $25,000-plus of credit card debt at 24% — that difference can be thousands of dollars.

So which one? Here's my honest answer after coaching real people through this: **the best method is the one you'll actually finish.** The avalanche wins the spreadsheet. The snowball wins the human being. If you're disciplined and the highest-rate debt isn't so big it'll take two years to clear, run the avalanche and pocket the savings. If you've started and quit before, or you need to *feel* progress to stay in it, run the snowball. A plan you complete beats a "smarter" plan you abandon every single time.

## The Plan: How to Pay Off Debt Fast, Step by Step

Method aside, the execution is the same. Here's the exact sequence I walk people through.

1. **List every debt in one place.** Balance, minimum payment, interest rate — all of it, on one page. This is the step people dread most, and it's the one that changes everything. You can't beat a number you refuse to look at.
2. **Build a $1,000 starter buffer first.** Before you attack the debt, park $1,000 in a separate account. Without it, the next flat tire goes straight back on a credit card and you're stuck in the loop. A small buffer keeps life's surprises from undoing your progress.
3. **Find your extra dollars.** Every payoff plan runs on the gap between what you earn and what you spend. Assign every dollar a job with a real budget, and you'll usually find $200 to $500 a month hiding in subscriptions, takeout, and untracked spending.
4. **Pick your target and concentrate fire.** Snowball or avalanche — choose, then throw every extra dollar at the top of the list while paying minimums on the rest. One target at a time.
5. **Roll the payment forward, never back into your life.** When a debt dies, take its entire payment and stack it onto the next one. This is where "fast" comes from: your payoff power grows with every account you close, even though your income never changed.

Do this and the math flips. Instead of interest compounding against you, your payment size compounds *for* you. The last debts fall faster than the first ones — that's the snowball rolling downhill.

## What's Actually at Stake

Let's be clear about the cost of doing nothing. Carry the balances and you don't just lose the interest — you lose options. You can't take the risk, leave the job, or weather the slow season, because every month your money is already spoken for before it arrives. Debt is quiet, but it runs your life from the background.

Now picture the other side. No credit card payment. No car loan. Every dollar you earn is *yours*, free to go toward the future instead of the past. That's not a fantasy — it's just the far end of a plan you start today. The people I've watched get there aren't smarter or richer than you. They just stopped guessing and started sequencing.

You already know how to work. This is about making sure that work finally buys you freedom instead of just covering interest.
